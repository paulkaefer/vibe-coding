Under the hood: k-means clustering

Every pixel is a point in RGB space. K-means repeats two steps until nothing changes: (1) assign each point to its nearest centroid, (2) move each centroid to the mean of its points. Centroids start at farthest-point positions, so results are repeatable.

Elbow: choosing k

Inertia (WCSS) is the total squared distance from each point to its centroid. It always falls as k grows; look for the "elbow" where extra clusters stop paying off. The filled dot is your current k.

Segmentation: clusters in the image

Each pixel is repainted with its cluster's centroid color. It follows the iteration slider. Note that k-means only sees color, never position, so distant pixels of similar color share a cluster.
